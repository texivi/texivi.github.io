Cardinals

A "cardinal" is a number that tells you how big a set is. An ordinal tells you where something is in an ordering, and a cardinal tells you how many things there are. omega keeps cardinals as their own type, seperate from ordinals and from ordinary numbers.

What they are

Two sets have the same size when their members can be paired off one to one. Pair each natural number n with the even number 2n and every member of both sets gets exactly one partner, so there are as many even numbers as natural numbers. That size is ℵ₀, and omega agrees:

aleph0 aleph0 + .       ℵ₀
aleph0 aleph0 * .       ℵ₀

Positions and sizes split apart here. ω, ω+1 and ω·2 are different places in the line, but each one has only countably many places before it, so all three have size ℵ₀:

omega 2 * card .        ℵ₀

Some sets are bigger. Cantor's diagonal argument shows that the subsets of the natural numbers cannot be paired off with the natural numbers, so 2^ℵ₀, the continuum, is larger than ℵ₀. diagonal runs the argument on screen (demos). ℵ₁ is the next size after ℵ₀, and whether it equals the continuum is the continuum hypothesis, which omega refuses to answer (see below).

The pages aleph-null, countable-sets, hilbert-hotel and uncountable-sets go through the same ideas in more detail. Vsauce's How To Count Past Infinity explains all of this with pictures.

Literals

Word Value Printed
aleph0 the size of the natural numbers ℵ₀
aleph1 the next infinite size ℵ₁
n aleph the n-th infinite size ℵₙ
continuum the size of the real numbers, 2^ℵ₀ 2^ℵ₀ 
3 aleph .               ℵ₃
12 aleph .              ℵ_12

aleph takes any non-negative 64-bit number. Above 9 the index is printed after an underscore, since the font has subscript digits only up to 9. The index has to be a finite number, so ℵ_ω and the alephs above it cannot be written in omega, even though set theory has them.

The other values omega can represent are powers of two: 2 n aleph ^ is 2^ℵₙ.

Addition and multiplication

For infinite cardinals, sums and products are the larger of the two:

aleph0 aleph0 + .       ℵ₀
aleph0 aleph0 * .       ℵ₀
3 aleph0 + .            ℵ₀
aleph0 aleph1 + .       ℵ₁
aleph0 continuum * .    2^ℵ₀
0 aleph1 * .            0

Adding a finite number to an infinite cardinal does not change it, and multiplying by 0 gives 0. When the larger of the two cannot be determined in set theory, the operation fails with the ZFC error described below: aleph2 continuum + is either ℵ₂ or 2^ℵ₀, and ZFC cannot say which.

Powers

Only these powers are supported:

Form Result
n aleph_k ^, with n ≥ 2 finite 2^ℵₖ
aleph_k n ^, with n ≥ 1 finite ℵₖ 
2 aleph0 ^ .            2^ℵ₀
10 aleph0 ^ .           2^ℵ₀
aleph0 3 ^ .            ℵ₀
2 aleph0 ^ continuum = .    1

Every finite base from 2 up gives the same power, since nᵏ and 2ᵏ have the same size for infinite k. A cardinal raised to a finite power is itself.

Everything else, such as aleph0 aleph0 ^, fails with an error. Several of these have answers in ZFC (ℵ₀^ℵ₀ is 2^ℵ₀), but omega only implements the two rules above.

Comparison

aleph0 aleph1 < .               1
aleph0 continuum < .            1
aleph1 continuum <= .           1
2 aleph0 ^ 2 aleph1 ^ <= .      1

Comparisons follow what ZFC can prove:

  • ℵⱼ < ℵₖ exactly when j < k.
  • ℵₖ < 2^ℵₖ always, by Cantor's theorem. The diagonal demo shows the proof (demos).
  • ℵₖ₊₁ ≤ 2^ℵₖ always, but whether they are equal is undecidable.
  • 2^ℵⱼ ≤ 2^ℵₖ for j < k, but whether the inequality is strict is undecidable.
  • ℵⱼ compared with 2^ℵₖ for j > k+1 is undecidable either way.

Every finite number is smaller than every infinite cardinal.

The continuum hypothesis

The continuum hypothesis says that no size lies strictly between the natural numbers and the real numbers, that is, aleph1 equals continuum.

fails with

ZFC can't decide this one (continuum hypothesis, Godel 1940 and Cohen 1963)

The hypothesis is independent of ZFC: Gödel showed in 1940 that ZFC cannot disprove it, and Cohen showed in 1963 that ZFC cannot prove it. omega does not assume an answer. A comparison whose result depends on it, or on the generalised hypothesis for higher alephs, fails with the same message, and since failing fails the line, nothing a program computes ever rests on an assumption it did not make.

Comparisons that come out the same either way still work. aleph1 continuum <= is 1, and so is aleph1 continuum > not.

Ordinals and cardinals

Ordinals and cardinals cannot be mixed directly:

omega aleph0 +          can't mix cardinals with that

Convert an ordinal with card first:

omega 2 * card .        ℵ₀
epsilon0 card .         ℵ₀
omega1 card .           ℵ₁

Finite numbers work with both types.

Subtraction and division fail with cardinals only support + * and ^.

Saving

Cardinals are saved as source, 0 aleph for ℵ₀ and 2 0 aleph ^ for 2^ℵ₀, and read back exactly (saving).
